## Summary

- Scope changed during the card (Nick via coordinator, 2026-11-08): the near-term target is 2-4 games held for a few minutes, matching the simulator live. 50-100 per 10 min across every game is brain-phase work; it appears here as one summary row.
- NFL, 2 sticks, 60-90 s behind, 0 mid-play joins in every row: today's sticks 1.7-1.9 jumps per 10 min, skip-the-huddle with seek 8 s and prep 15 s 12.7 / 14.7 / 15.0 (2 / 3 / 4 games).
- The ceiling at today's position error is the clip: 27 s visible + 2.5 s flip = 20.3 per 10 min on one TV. With a parked-stick error of 0.5 s sd the same setup reaches 16.3 / 20.7 / 23.1.
- NBA reaches 1.9-2.3 (3.5-4.3 at 0.5 s error): a live stretch must run to the next dead ball, so clips are long. 50 per 10 min was not reached in any row, including all games on 4 sticks (NFL best 38.9).
- Tonight's live test must measure five stick numbers (see P1-MAXRATE-04). Seek latency and seek failure rate are what separate 2 per 10 min from 13-15.

## Model

`python scripts/buffered_jump_sim.py --maxrate-sweep --archive data/archive/nfl --json out.json` reproduces every row (about 1.5 minutes on one core). `--maxrate` with the `--mr-*` flags runs one combination. Neither flag touches the editor sim or any live default; `redzone/decider/buffered.py` is unchanged.

The TV is one machine showing one clip at a time. Each hidden stick takes one job: park at a play's landing position, by seeking inside its game (`seek_s`, plus up to one 15 s YouTube TV step rolled at 1x) or by moving to another game (`prep_s`). A job is known only once the play-by-play has it (feed lag 20 +/- 6 s), and it must air inside Nick's 60-90 s window, so each play has a release and a deadline. When nothing is ready the picture rolls on, and holds on a frame rather than enter a commercial. Rule #1 is checked against truth at every landing and leave: true position = parked position + a per-seek error (sd `err_sd`, 1.5 s, the editor sim's value). Margins are 4.5 sd each side.

Definitions: a jump is an input flip. Clip = the visible landing-to-leave length. `strict` truth is the repo's Rule #1 span (entry wallclock - 10 s to + 3 s). `tight` truth is an assumed real snap-to-whistle (mean 6.5 s, sd 2, 3-12 s) with a snap detector we do not have. Games at a time: N live games for `block_s` (300 s), then the next N. Basketball lands in a dead ball or the walk after a shot and leaves only in a dead ball (`cuts.walks`).

Calibration: the same slates through the existing editor sim give 8.3 cuts per 10 min (NFL, 7+ games); the model's closest row (rolls 1x, prep 60, 2-4 games) gives 4.7-5.5. The model is conservative because it always shows a whole padded clip, so treat its rows as a floor for that setup and compare rows with each other. NBA: editor sim 1.1, model 0.5-0.7.

### P1-MAXRATE-03: what limits the rate

| Limit | Numbers | What moves it |
|---|---|---|
| Stick failures | 74% scrub failure, 40 s seek: 1.7-2.0 | Scrub reliability (P1-BOTTLENECKS-03) |
| Hidden stick cannot skip | Rolls 1x: 4.7-5.5 | Same-game seek of 8-15 s: 11-15 |
| Supply on 2 games | 16.0 plays per 10 min (2 games); 12.7 reached | More games at a time: 3 or 4 |
| Clip length (position error) | 27 s clip caps one TV at 20.3; 0.5 s error gives an 18 s clip and cap 29.3 | Better position knowledge |
| One TV | 50 per 10 min needs a 12 s cycle: 9.5 s visible at a 2.5 s flip | Snap known to well under 1 s; brain phase |

- Time on each game barely matters: staying 2, 5 or 10 minutes gives 13.1, 12.7, 12.8 (NFL, 2 games).
- Prep matters less than seek when 2-4 games stay put: seek 8 with prep 30 vs prep 15 is 11.2 vs 12.7 (2 games).
- Replays add jumps only by leaving the window: about 5.6 of the 18.0 jumps per 10 min air more than 90 s behind. WORTH FIXING: no (measurement).

## What to change in this repo

Nothing live. The flags are in `scripts/buffered_jump_sim.py`, tests in `tests/test_maxrate_sim.py`. For the brain phase: `run_maxrate` takes one `MR` and a slate, and `MR_GRID` holds the rows, so a new policy or measured stick numbers add one row each. The job selection is the `assign` function (`policy` picks fifo or edf).

## Risks / unverified

- Every stick number in the table is a parameter. Only flip, tune and scrub times are measured (P1-BOTTLENECKS); seek latency for a hidden 15 s step has never been measured alone.
- The archive has no snap times. The tight-dwell rows rest on an assumed play length and a detector that does not exist.
- Prime is not modelled separately: it enters through `prep_s` and `seek_s`. Whether Prime can seek in steps at all is unknown, so tonight must check it.
- Mid-play leaves are not zero: 232 across all rows (at most 10 in a row, out of thousands of jumps), from position error on a rolling picture. Mid-play joins are 0 in every row at 4.5 sd; at 4 sd a few rows showed 1.
- Ad seconds are at most 0.11 per 10 min, not exactly 0: the same position-error tail at a frozen picture.
- The picture holds a frozen frame when nothing is ready, which is dead air the model does not penalize. A failed seek or prep costs one repeat of itself, so the failure rows are a lower bound on the loss.
- NFL and NBA only; the block rotation is a simple round-robin of live games.
