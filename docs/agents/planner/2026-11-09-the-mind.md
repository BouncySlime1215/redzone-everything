# The mind: what the brain's models should actually be

## Answer in 10 lines

1. **The mind is a stack of small models, not one big one.** Gradient-boosted trees estimate value, code schedules the facts, and a small LLM writes words overnight. Every output fits Planner 2's `BrainOutput`.
2. **The first model worth building is GameValue:** one small tree model per league that estimates the must-see value of each game in the 3 minutes past the look-ahead. It chooses which 2-4 games to hold.
3. **It replaces the guess at the hidden stick.** It goes live first through the preload rung of Planner 3's ladder, where a wrong answer costs a less useful preload and cannot cause a mid-play cut.
4. **Measured here on dev nights:** holding 4 NFL games, a plain lookup table of clock, margin and field position captures 71.8% of next-3-minute must-see value. Closest-score-first captures 60.0%, random 56.4%, hindsight 96.2%.
5. **Most of that gap to hindsight cannot be learned.** About 90% of the variance in NFL next-3-minute value is event noise. The estimated ceiling for any model of any size is R² about 0.10, and the table is already at 0.078.
6. **Headroom differs sharply by league.** NBA has real room (table 0.37, ceiling about 0.67, because crunch is forecastable). NHL has essentially none (ceiling about 0.00). So NBA gets the most model effort and NHL gets a rule.
7. **"Billions of data points" is honest only as row counts.** The real information is about 0.1 B unique events from tens of thousands of games. Billions of rows mostly repeat those. They do pay off in rare tails, such as the 2% restart bound.
8. **Billions of parameters is the wrong spend.** Compute-optimal for our corpus is about 40 M parameters. A 1 B pretrain needs about 20 B unique tokens, 25x what exists, and 74-111 weeks of free T4. A 3 B pretrain at published scale is $442k-$885k.
9. **On the Mac:** live models stay under about 100 MB total. No LLM during games in v1, because this 8 GB Mac had 6.4 GB of swap in use when measured. A 1.7 B model runs at 4 AM as the "morning meeting" and writes each game's storyline card.
10. **It improves weekly, not nightly, as a model.** One Sunday is about 0.4% of NFL history. Each night instead updates the measured numbers (feed lag, stick speed), scores the current model on truly unseen games, and mines the night's worst decisions.

### 2. Measured here: how much can any model win?

Script: `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-nick-matta-Library-Application-Support-Claude-scratch-workspaces-9fd867a4-1593-493b-b94f-1f3aebd68493-688aed4e-2f6f-4b8a-9ed9-aa097c310a36-scratch-2026-11-08-ce8f23/9f563e62-788a-4e8b-a325-b193c8b21949/scratchpad/mind/holdset.py` and `ceil.py` in the same folder (not in the repo; read-only, about 3 s, no training).

Setup:

- Dev nights only. Locked and fresh nights were excluded using Planner 1's cutoffs (NFL before 20251209, NBA and NHL before 20261029). College and MLB were not used.
- Every 180 s on each slate, every live game gets a target: tier-weighted must-see in the **next 180 s**, using Planner 1's detectors and weights T1=5, T2=2, T3=0.5 (weights are mine; decision 2).
- The clock `t` is the feed head, so this window is exactly what lies past the look-ahead.
- Whole-night time split: oldest 60% of dev nights fit a shrunken lookup table (period, clock band, margin band, NFL field position), newest 40% test it.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random | 56.4 | 59.2 | 53.1 | 38.0 | 41.9 | 44.8 |
| Closest score first | 60.0 | 80.9 | 50.7 | 41.4 | 62.6 | 43.5 |

Train and test: NFL 26 and 18 nights (208 games), NBA 5 and 4 (68), NHL 6 and 4 (66). NBA and NHL are thin.

**Noise ceiling.** For a compound Poisson count, the variance that remains even when the true rate is known has mean `E[sum of w²]`. Subtract it from the observed variance and what is left is the most any model could explain. Scoring tempo closely following a Poisson process is the published finding for football, hockey and basketball ([b1](#b1)). Tier events are broader than scores, so this is an approximation.

| | NFL | NBA | NHL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variance of target | 4.67 | 9.36 | 3.28 |
| Poisson noise part | 4.21 | 3.08 | 3.27 |
| **Estimated R² ceiling, any model** | **0.099** | **0.671** | **0.002** |
| Table's R² on test nights | 0.078 | 0.366 | 0.016 |
| Share of windows with zero value | 45% | 40% | 39% |
| Table R² fit on 25% / 50% / 100% of train nights | 0.044 / 0.060 / 0.078 | 0.150 / 0.328 / 0.366 | 0.002 / 0.012 / 0.016 |

What this says:

- **Hindsight is not a target past the look-ahead.** The 96% includes knowing which drive scores. Only code, inside the look-ahead, gets that.
- **NFL:** state alone takes most of what is available. Field position is doing the work: red-zone games are obvious. A tree model should add a little; a large model has almost nothing left to find on this target.
- **NBA:** real headroom, about 9 points at K=4 (83 to 92). The reason is structural: Tier 1 is defined by late and close, so "which game will reach crunch" is forecastable and worth forecasting.
- **NHL:** the next 3 minutes are noise. Ship a rule (power play, late one-goal, OT) and spend nothing on a live value model.
- **The learning curves still rise at these tiny sizes.** History will help. It helps up to the ceiling, then stops.

Limits, stated plainly:

- The ceiling assumes events are independent given the rate. Clustering, such as a red-zone trip and a touchdown in the same window, means the true ceiling is likely lower still.
- "Simulated true rate" is a model and carries several points of error: NHL's table (50.6) beats its simulated ceiling (42.9) at K=2.
- "Closest score" is a stand-in. The real `standby.Planner` and `engine.closeness` were not run. Step 1 does that.
- A held game is not automatically a seen event. Capture here is the ceiling for a hold-set choice, not what the sticks deliver.

### 3. The architecture

Three speeds, one interface (Planner 2's `think(BrainInput) -> BrainOutput`).

| `BrainOutput` field | v1 source | Type | When it runs | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `values[].value_next_3m` | **GameValue**: LightGBM per league, Tweedie objective ([b6](#b6)) | Trees | On new play or changed options | About 120 KB per head |
| `values[].p_tier1_60s` | GameValue second head, binary with isotonic calibration | Trees | Same | About 120 KB per head |
| `values[].value_now` | **Code:** tier value already known inside the look-ahead | Fact | Same | None |
| `preload` | **Code formula:** `value_next_3m x P(reachable given prep_s)` | Derived | Same | None |
| `scores` v1 | **Code formula:** known value inside L plus discounted `value_next_3m` | Derived | Same | None |
| `scores` v2 | LambdaRank on oracle value-to-go (PICK's successor), only if it beats v1 | Trees | Same | About 100 KB |
| `stay_one_more` | **Derived:** next-play value here vs best legal alternative | Derived | At dead balls | None |
| `why` | Code template from the top feature contributions plus the storyline card | Words | Same | None |

Plus three things outside that per-tick output:

| Model | Job | Runs | Size | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timing v1 (exists) | Restart lower bound | Inside code rule | About 120 KB each | Keep; add leagues |
| **Pre-game importance** | Which games matter tonight: filters 15+ game slates, sets the first hold-set | LightGBM once a day on as-of spread, total, Elo, standings stakes, rest, rivalry | About 100 KB | Second model |
| **Morning meeting** | Storyline card per game: why it matters, in words and bounded numbers | Existing 1.7 B Qwen3 student, 4 AM batch, Mac idle | 1.7 B, off during games | Third |

Why this shape for each task:

- **Trees for numbers.** Our inputs are numeric game state with about 20 to 40 features. Trees remain the default for tabular data of this kind ([b2](#b2)), and the repo's own trees run at 0.43 ms for 12 games.
- **Tweedie, not plain regression.** The target has 40-45% zeros and is a sum of weighted events. That is the distribution Tweedie is built for ([b6](#b6)).
- **Why GameValue rather than another PICK.** PICK imitates the DP's next game, needs whole-night oracle runs, exists for NBA only and has plateaued. GameValue's label needs only one game's play-by-play and the tier list, so every game with a wall clock counts, it has no compounding-error problem, and it answers the question Planner 2 assigned this planner.
- **Why not a sequence model first.** The ceiling above applies to every model class. A sequence model might find NBA texture (runs, foul trouble) that tabular features miss, so it stays a challenger: NFM-S at 25 M parameters costs about 6 free T4-hours, and EXP-103 is already pinned. It ships only by beating GameValue on the same folds. Published play-level LLM work reports about 64% next-pitch accuracy on about 3 B tokens ([b5](#b5)), which is a research result in one sport, not evidence for switching.
- **Why no live LLM in v1.** Live "storylines" that matter are numbers code can compute from play-by-play: run in progress, comeback size, star's line, stakes. A language model adds wording, and wording can wait until morning.

Cross-league comparison comes from the label. A Tier 1 is worth the same points in every league, so `value_next_3m` is on one scale by construction. Leverage-excitement's per-league percentile applies only if suspense is added as a feature. League order stays a code tiebreak.

**The four existing value models** (Planner 2 asked for this):

| Model | Verdict |
|---|---|
| `noggin/predict.py` (Predict v1) | Retire as a separate model. `p_tier1_60s` replaces it. Off today |

### 9. Evaluation without leakage

**Three rings, all whole-night and time-ordered, per league:**

| Ring | Nights | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Public seasons | Factory train, calib, test (NBA 1,967 / 421 / 421 nights; NFL 571 / 122 / 122) | Forward-chaining folds, mean and worst fold, all tuning |
| 2. Dev archive | Through the real grader and sim | Does the value survive the mechanics |
| 3. Locked once, then fresh | `splits.py` locked block, then every night after the training cutoff | Release only |

**Metrics, in order:** hold-set capture at K=2 and K=4; `p_tier1_60s` ECE at or under 0.10; Tier 1 seen and `pct_of_oracle` through the sim; 0 mid-play entries. Report by slice: crunch, OT, your team, blowout, slate size.

**Bars for GameValue v1 (proposed, on Ring 1 worst fold and Ring 2):**

| League | Bar |
|---|---|
| NFL | At least the state table (71.8 at K=4 on this dev split), and above the real `standby.Planner` |
| NBA | K=4 at least 88, midway between the table and the simulated ceiling |
| NHL | No model. The rule must beat random at K=2 |
| CFB, CBB, MLB | Pending: set after a table baseline exists |

**Leakage list specific to this work:**

1. **Published WP columns.** nflfastR's WP model was fit across seasons, so a 2015 row's WP has seen later seasons. It is also absent on the live feed. Use as-of WP or the feed's own.
2. **Season-long team strength.** Elo and records must be as of that morning. Same for standings stakes.
3. **Cleaned history vs live feed.** Archives are corrected after the game; the live feed is late, duplicated and revised. Train with sampled feed lag, not the fixed p90, and run the 10% drop and 3 s jitter slices.
4. **Synthetic slates.** Carry `source_night_id` on every row. CI fails if a source night crosses splits (training log, now).
5. **Same game, several sources.** EXP-721's canonical `game_key` is required.
6. **Learning the locked set through thresholds.** Planner 1 set bands on all CFB, CBB and MLB nights, so their locked blocks must use later nights only. Fix those blocks before the first training run.
7. **Tuning budget:** log trials per model comparison (training log).

Locked power is weak: 4 NBA and 4 NHL nights. Ring 1's 421-night test sets carry the statistical weight, and Ring 3 confirms direction and safety. The process planner should decide whether eval-data-v2 comes before any 95% claim.

### 10. How it improves from each night

| Cadence | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Every night, 5 AM | Archive the night. Re-run the logged `BrainInput`s for skew. Score the current model on these unseen games. Update the measured tables (feed lag, stick speed, delay) | These are the numbers that drift. Feed lag is a 2-day sample with NFL n=150 |
| Every night | Top 10 regrets: where GameValue most underrated what happened beyond noise. Output is a list for an agent to review, with no automatic change | Finds bugs and missing features |
| Weekly | Kaggle CPU retrains candidates on everything through last week. Candidate goes to shadow | One Sunday is 13-14 games against 3,811: about 0.4% |
| At most monthly | Promotion after shadow 7 of 7 paired nights (or 10 of 12), per noggin.md gates | No live retraining, as Planner 2 requires |
| Each new season | Re-measure rule eras and tier rates. CBB in March needs its own check | Planner 1 |

Each night is automatically an honest test of any model trained earlier, which is a stronger guarantee than any stored holdout and costs nothing.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A model graded before the sticks can act looks useless** (PICK, Predict) | Grade at Ring 1 for the model and Ring 2 after the queue work lands. Don't reject a model on today's 1.8 jumps per 10 min |
| 2 | **My ceiling numbers are a small sample and an approximation** | Step 1 repeats them on public seasons, by league, before any model is built |
| 3 | **Tier weights set the whole value function** and are taste | Decision 2; report a sensitivity run with 3/2/1 |
| 4 | **Noise-chasing.** With 90% noise, a model can look better in a fold by luck | Worst fold, night-level bootstrap, and a bar against the table rather than against random |
| 5 | **Train-serve skew.** Archive plays are cleaned | One feature definition, log `BrainInput`, jitter slices |
| 6 | **The label ignores story.** A dull blowout with big plays scores high | Garbage-time weighting in code, and importance as a separate input. Don't conclude a story is worthless because this metric can't see it |
| 7 | **No ground truth for storylines** under the self-taught rule | Keep them bounded and off the safety path. Attention counts could serve as a secondary check |
| 8 | **Value only pays when held games get seen** | Planner 3's queue is the prerequisite. Stick throughput decides what the hold-set is worth |
| 9 | **Factory state unknown** (no ledger found) | Step 2 begins by confirming what landed on Kaggle |
| 10 | **League data thin or changed** (CBB in March, CFB one Saturday, MLB era) | Pending bars, not guessed ones |
