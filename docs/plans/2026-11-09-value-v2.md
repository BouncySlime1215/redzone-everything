> **Superseded by [docs/PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)**, the only plan (section 4 points here). This file is the Value v2 spec it uses.

Nick, Nov 9: "that lacks criteria… its sports… its all gotta be fucking dialed."

Value v1 is 3 or 4 kinds of moment per sport with fixed points (5 / 2 / 0.5). A garbage-time touchdown in a 38-3 game
scores the same as a go-ahead touchdown with 0:40 left. That is the baseline this replaces.

## The shape

A game's value over the next 3 minutes has two parts:

- **Payoff:** what happened. Each moment = `base × leverage × stakes`.
- **Suspense:** what might happen. `P(big moment soon | game state) × what it would be worth`. 1st and goal at the 3 is
  worth watching before anything happens; v1 gives it 2 points and nothing for the snap count.

Two more multipliers apply when the brain picks, not in the training label, because they are personal:

- **You:** Washington and UNC, a live bet this moment decides, a fantasy player involved, teams taught on the phone.
- **League order:** NFL > CFB > NBA > NCAA basketball > NHL > MLB, equal when both are in crunch.

And one subtraction: **dead value**. Commercials, reviews that decide nothing, kneel-downs, intentional walks, non-clutch
free throws and garbage time score below zero, so staying on them costs.

## 2. Suspense: states worth watching before anything happens

The model learns `P(payoff in the next 3 min | state)`; these are the states the label must be able to see.

- **Football:** goal-to-go; 1st down inside the 20; 4th down and going; two-minute drill in a one-score game; last
  possession down 8 or less; field-goal range with under a minute; overtime.
- **Basketball:** last 2 minutes, margin 5 or less; one possession left in a one-possession game; a shooter at the line
  down 1 to 3 with under 10 s.
- **Hockey:** goalie pulled; power play in the 3rd period of a one-goal game; last 2 minutes of a one-goal game;
  3-on-3 overtime; shootout.
- **Baseball:** bases loaded; runner on third, under 2 outs; full count with 2 outs and runners on; tying run at the
  plate, 8th inning on; no-hitter from the 6th.

## 3. Leverage: how much the moment mattered to the result (×0.3 to ×3.0)

- From our own win-probability model per league, fit only on seasons before the one being scored (the published ones
  were fit across seasons, which is the leak the code already refuses).
- Leverage = how much the moment's outcome moves win probability, scaled so an average play is 1.0.
- Garbage time (one side past 97% late) floors at 0.3. One-score, last 2 minutes or overtime caps at 3.0.
- Until the model exists: a margin-by-time table per league, the same idea `redzone/lab/leverage.py` already uses.

## 4. Stakes: how much the game mattered (×1.0 to ×2.0)

| Stake | × | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Elimination or championship game | 2.0 | ESPN season type and round |
| Tournament game (March Madness, playoffs) | 1.5 | ESPN season type |
| Clinch or elimination on the line tonight | 1.5 | standings |
| Upset brewing: underdog of 7+ leading or within one score late | 1.5 | pre-game spread in the summary |
| Ranked vs ranked (college) | 1.4 | rankings in the scoreboard |
| Rivalry | 1.3 | a fixed list per league |
| Record or milestone chase in this game | 1.5 | player season and career totals |

## 5. Where the numbers come from, so they are not my guesses

1. **What NFL RedZone actually aired** on non-locked Sundays (`scripts/redzone_reconstruct.py`): fit football weights so
   our ranking of moments matches what the real show chose.
2. **Official highlight lists:** each league publishes which plays made the recap. A play in the recap is a label of
   "worth showing", for every sport, in the hundreds of thousands.
3. **Suspense and surprise from win probability** (Ely, Frankel and Kamenica, 2015): suspense is how much the outcome is
   about to move, surprise is how much it just moved. Both come free from section 3's model.
4. **Nick:** the teach button and taps outrank everything above for his own screen.

That is also how the count reaches the hundreds of thousands: about 40 kinds per sport × leverage × stakes × game
state × who is watching, with fitted weights, not 40 hand-set numbers.
