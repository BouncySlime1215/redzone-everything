# Train the world model fast: portable trainer, scaling ladder, MoE

Owner: lane 1. Order: Nick 12:05 AM Nov 5 ("compute is the bottleneck; how do we train this thing fast").
Serves: North Star "crunch live %" and "dead air %" through the world model. Pre-registered: EXP-106, EXP-107 and
EXP-108 in docs/experiments.md. Everything trained on plan B data is **personal-use, not for commercial release**.

## One trainer, four places

`scripts/nfm_train.py` is the same file everywhere; the backend is picked at start.

| where | how it runs | who launches |
|---|---|---|
| Kaggle TPU v5e-8 (~20 h/week free, 9 h per session) | `colab/nfm_tpu_kaggle.ipynb` on kaggle-inputs; torch_xla, SPMD data parallel over 8 chips; checkpoints to /kaggle/working every 30 min; Run all again resumes | Nick |
| Colab A100 | `colab/nfm_a100.ipynb`; CUDA, bf16 autocast, SDPA (flash kernel) | Nick |
| UNC Longleaf (SLURM) | `sbatch scripts/slurm/nfm_train.sbatch` (PRESET=300m to override); resubmit to resume | Nick, under his own account |
| GCP TPU VM (TRC) | `PJRT_DEVICE=TPU python scripts/nfm_train.py --build-vocab 2 ...` | Nick |

What makes it fast: sequence packing (games back to back, no padding), bf16, RoPE + SDPA (flash on GPU, fused on
XLA), vocab padded to a multiple of 128, static shapes everywhere (fixed batch x ctx, capacity-based MoE), and the
whole data stream in host memory (~0.6 GB for 310M tokens). Grading runs once, off the TPU, on the best checkpoint.

## Ladder (EXP-107) and go / no-go

Params are from `count_params` (tests pin them). The tokens are about 310M unique public v2 tokens: NFL 2001-24,
NBA cdn 2021-25 and MLB Retrosheet 1974-2024. Every point trains on the same 1.2B-token budget.

| preset | non-embedding | total | train FLOPs | A100 h (~40% MFU) | TPU v5e-8 h |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 18m | 14M | 22M | 1.6e17 | 0.35 | 0.07 |
| 60m | 61M | 74M | 5.3e17 | 1.2 | 0.25 |
| 150m (Saturday) | 156M | 174M | 1.25e18 | 2.8 | 0.55 |
| 300m | 319M | 342M | 2.5e18 | 5.5 | 1.1 |

**Go to 1B+ only if** valid loss falls by at least 0.02 nats from 150m to 300m, and the fitted L(N) = E + A/N^alpha
predicts at least 0.02 more at 1B. Above roughly 15M params the ladder is data-constrained: the crews' new leagues
(StatsBomb about 260M v2 tokens, plus MiLB, wehoop and cricket) raise the unique token count before a 1B run.

## MoE (EXP-108 now, moe-5b later)

- `moe-60m`: 8 experts, top-2, half-width SwiGLU experts. That is 198M total and 74M active, the same active size as
  dense 60m. Graded against dense at equal tokens.
- `moe-5b`: d 2048, 22 layers, 12 experts, top-2. That is **4.98B total and 1.17B active**, ready for when big compute
  lands (TRC v4/v5 pods, Longleaf multi-GPU, NAIRR). Routing uses a fixed capacity factor of 1.25 with a Switch-style
  balance loss of 0.01. The router is dense einsum dispatch, so XLA sees static shapes.
- Expert parallelism and the 2-D mesh (data x model) are the next step, once the 5B run has a pod to run on.

## Throughput test (EXP-106)

The same seed, preset and data run on TPU and on A100. Results land in grades.json under `throughput`:
- tokens/s and tokens/s per chip;
- achieved TFLOPs;
- the first 20 steps are excluded.

The correctness bar is valid loss within 2% at step 1,000. The A100 baseline is EXP-100's 121,973 tok/s at 18.6M on
an L4.

## Risks

- Kaggle's TPU image may not ship torch_xla for its torch build. The notebook pip-installs the matching wheel first.
  If that fails, the fallback is a JAX port, about a day of work.
- Retrosheet's ~1,500 small files make the MLB data build the slow step, about 25 min. The shards are cached under
  /kaggle/working, so a resume skips the build.
